Dave(Hyeonjung)Kim

Ph.D. STUDENT | ROBOTICS (DRoM), SYSTEMS & CONTROL @ UCLA

I'm a Mechanical Engineering Ph.D. student at UCLA with a focus on Robotics (DRoM), Systems & Control, where I am fortunate to be co-advised by Prof. Clites and Prof. Hopkins. My research spans the full-stack design of bioinspired robots, from mechanism design to control for safe physical interaction and robot learning for dexterous manipulation. The central question of my Ph.D. is how to optimally design and control a robot hand that can safely and dexterously interact with unstructured environments.

Before doing research at UCLA, I graduated Summa Cum Laude from Yonsei University. I have experience working on the Jump Enhancing Textile-Suit at Yonsei’s HCAIR Lab, and the Dual-Transmission Actuator for prosthetics at Seoul National University’s Biorobotics Lab. At Yonsei Artificial Intelligence, I worked on sim-to-real of IMU-free bipedal humanoids and a zero-demonstration VLA fine-tuning method using reinforcement-learned rollouts for the SO-101. Across these projects I have worked full-stack, from mechanical design and low-level control to learning-based methods, with hands-on experience in SolidWorks, PyTorch, Isaac Lab, and MATLAB.

Reinforced-Tensegrity Joints for Safe, Dexterous Robot Hands
Hyeonjung Kim, advised by Tyler Clites and Jonathan Hopkins
A tendon-driven robot hand built on reinforced-tensegrity joints, compliant joints that stay soft for safe contact, stiffen on demand for force, and switch quickly between the two. Two tracks run in parallel, the hand itself and extending the joints' fatigue life under cyclic load toward implant-level durability.

A Soft Wearable Robot for Vertical Jump Enhancement via a Pneumatic Energy-Storing Propulsion Actuator and Triarticular Kinetic-Chained Structure
Sunghun Kim, Hyeokjun Seo, Hyeonjung Kim, Sengryeol Lee, Namho Kim, Dongjun Shin
We present the Jump Enhancing Textile (JET)-Suit, a soft wearable exoskeleton that increases the user's vertical jump height through a Pneumatic Energy-Storing Actuator (PESPA) and a Triarticular Kinetic-Chained Structure (TKiCS).
DOI
Soft wearable robots have garnered increasing attention across various fields; however, they remain insufficient in overcoming the physical limitations of the human body. In particular, enhancing vertical jump height, a commonly used indicator of physical capability, requires improving actuator power density and the efficiency of soft structures. To address these challenges, this study proposes the Jump-Enhancing Textile Suit (JET Suit), which integrates the Pneumatic Energy-Storing Propulsion Actuator (PESPA) and the Triarticular Kinetic-Chained Structure (TKiCS). PESPA enhances propulsion through elastic energy storage and release, while TKiCS optimizes kinetic chain interactions for improved force transmission. Experimental validation reveals a statistically significant increase in vertical jump height, with an average improvement of 3.74 cm and a maximum of 9.04 cm, alongside increased joint torques at the hip, knee, and ankle. Under isotonic testing conditions, PESPA achieves a high power density of 2298.69 W/kg, outperforming commercial pneumatic actuators. Additionally, accurate force prediction using an MCK-based model enables precise actuation control for effective assistance. These findings lay a solid foundation for pneumatic wearable robotics, offering promising applications in jump augmentation, rehabilitation, and athletic performance enhancement.

JOSE: Reconstructing Privileged State from Joint History for Humanoid Motion Control
Hyeonjung Kim, Sunghyun Park
JOSE (Joint-Only State Estimator) is a recurrent estimator that reconstructs a teacher policy’s privileged state, including the base orientation and angular velocity normally read from an IMU, from a window of joint-encoder history, and feeds it to a frozen teacher that runs with no IMU in the control loop. Across three Adversarial-Motion-Prior motion-tracking teachers and a command-conditioned locomotion teacher, it halves the motion error of joint-only distillation and tracks commands nearly as well as ground-truth observations.

SCOPE: Steer or Fine-tune? A Cheap Test for What a Frozen VLA Can Already Do
Hyeonjung Kim, Seungmin Jeon, Jiwhan Bong
A cheap, demonstration-free, weight-free test of whether a frozen flow-matching VLA already has the latent competence for a task. SCOPE measures a low-dimensional causal noise subspace and searches it to decide whether to steer the frozen policy or fine-tune it.
