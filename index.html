Dave(Hyeonjung)Kim

Ph.D. STUDENT | ROBOTICS @ UCLA

I'm a Mechanical Engineering Ph.D. student at UCLA with a focus on Robotics, Systems & Control, where I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Clites. My research is mainly on bioinspired robotics, with a more specific focus on prosthetics, humanoid hands and wearable robots. The central question of my Ph.D. is how to optimally design and control a robot hand that can safely and dexterously interact with unstructured environments.

Before my Ph.D., I graduated Summa Cum Laude from Yonsei University, and I worked on the Jump Enhancing Textile-Suit at Yonsei’s HCAIR Lab, the Dual-Transmission Actuator for prosthetic hands at Seoul National University’s Biorobotics Lab, and both sim-to-real of IMU-free bipedal humanoids and a zero-demo VLA fine-tuning method using reinforcement-learned rollouts for the SO-101 at Yonsei Artificial Intelligence. Across these projects I worked the full stack from mechanical design to low-level control to learning-based approaches, with hands-on experience in SolidWorks, PyTorch, IsaacLab, and MATLAB.

Advanced Intelligent Systems · 2025
A Soft Wearable Robot for Vertical Jump Enhancement via a Pneumatic Energy-Storing Propulsion Actuator and Triarticular Kinetic-Chained Structure
Sunghun Kim, Hyeokjun Seo, Hyeonjung Kim, Sengryeol Lee, Namho Kim, Dongjun Shin
We present the Jump Enhancing Textile (JET)-Suit, a soft wearable exoskeleton that helps increases user's vertical jump height through a Pneumatic Energy-Storing Actuator (PESPA) and a Triarticular Kinetic-Chained Structure (TKiCS).
DOI
Soft wearable robots have garnered increasing attention across various fields; however, they remain insufficient in overcoming the physical limitations of the human body. In particular, enhancing vertical jump height, a commonly used indicator of physical capability, requires improving actuator power density and the efficiency of soft structures. To address these challenges, this study proposes the Jump-Enhancing Textile Suit (JET Suit), which integrates the Pneumatic Energy-Storing Propulsion Actuator (PESPA) and the Triarticular Kinetic-Chained Structure (TKiCS). PESPA enhances propulsion through elastic energy storage and release, while TKiCS optimizes kinetic chain interactions for improved force transmission. Experimental validation reveals a statistically significant increase in vertical jump height, with an average improvement of 3.74 cm and a maximum of 9.04 cm, alongside increased joint torques at the hip, knee, and ankle. Under isotonic testing conditions, PESPA achieves a high power density of 2298.69 W/kg, outperforming commercial pneumatic actuators. Additionally, accurate force prediction using an MCK-based model enables precise actuation control for effective assistance. These findings lay a solid foundation for pneumatic wearable robotics, offering promising applications in jump augmentation, rehabilitation, and athletic performance enhancement.
